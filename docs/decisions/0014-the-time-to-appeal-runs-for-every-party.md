# 0014. The time to appeal Massachusetts v. Oz runs for every party and can run past 2026-12-28

Date: 2026-11-04

Status: Accepted on 2026-11-04, decided by the owner

## Context

Decision record 0013, accepted on 2026-10-29, says under Consequences: "The government's time to appeal runs to
2026-12-28, 60 days after the judgment under Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4(a)(1)(B)". Rule 4(a)(1)(B) gives
those 60 days to every party when one of them is a United States agency, or a United States officer sued in an
official capacity, as CMS, HHS and the officials heading them are here. 28 U.S.C. 2107(b) says the same. The skill's
watch list and the Note of `medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated` said the time runs for any party, so the States,
against whom the court entered partial judgment on Count II, may appeal too. Both cited 0013 for it. A read-only check
on 2026-10-30 flagged the difference, and the finding was rejected: 0013's words are true of the government, and an
accepted record is superseded rather than edited (the delivery plan's section 2.2, Done entry). On 2026-11-04 the
owner agreed to supersede them.

Reading the whole of Rule 4(a) for this record found four ways the date can move: another party's notice, a motion of
a kind Rule 4(a)(4)(A) lists, an extension and a reopening. The watch list carries one such motion, under Federal Rule
of Civil Procedure 59(e), without its date, and neither it nor 0013 states the other three ways.

## Decision

The time to appeal Massachusetts v. Oz is read from the whole of Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4(a), for every
party:

- Any party may file a notice of appeal within 60 days after the judgment, entry 149, entered on 2026-10-29, so by
  Monday 2026-12-28 (Rule 4(a)(1)(B)).
- Once one party files a timely notice, any other party may file within 14 days after it, where that ends later
  than 2026-12-28 (Rule 4(a)(3)). A notice filed on 2026-12-28 would give the others until 2027-01-11.
- A timely motion of a kind Rule 4(a)(4)(A) lists, a motion to alter or amend the judgment under Federal Rule of
  Civil Procedure 59(e) among them, restarts the time for every party from the order disposing of the last such
  motion. Rule 59(e)'s 28 days end on Thanksgiving Day, a legal holiday, so the last day for that motion is Friday
  2026-11-27 (Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(a)(1)(C) and (a)(6)(A)), unless that day is declared a holiday or the
  clerk's office is inaccessible (Rule 6(a)(3) and (a)(6)(B) and (C)). The court may not extend it (Rule 6(b)(2)).
- The district court may extend the time for excusable neglect or good cause, on a motion filed no later than 30
  days after the time expires, which is 2027-01-27 where the time expires on 2026-12-28. The extension runs to no
  later than 30 days after the time expired or 14 days after the order granting it, whichever is later (Rule
  4(a)(5); 28 U.S.C. 2107(c)).
- The district court may reopen the time for 14 days for a party that did not get notice of the judgment within 21
  days of its entry, if no party would be prejudiced, on a motion filed within 180 days after the entry or within 14
  days after the party gets notice, whichever is earlier, so by Tuesday 2027-04-27 at the latest (Rule 4(a)(6);
  28 U.S.C. 2107(c)).

So 2026-12-28 is the day to look, and no date closes the question for certain. A check on or after that day records
what the docket held when it was queried, as 0007 has every check do, and does not say that the ruling can no longer
be appealed.

This record supersedes the words of 0013 quoted above. The rest of 0013 stands, its clause on a stay pending appeal
included.

## Consequences

0013's status names this record.

The skill's watch list said the time to appeal "ends for any party" on 2026-12-28, citing 0013, and gave no date for a
motion under Rule 59(e). Its two items on the time to appeal, the two rows whose Recheck said "the time to appeal
ends" on 2026-12-28, and the project design's sentence on the time to appeal each gain a dated correction citing this
record, in the same commit. The rows' Notes keep 2026-12-28 as the time to appeal, the date that holds unless
something on the docket moves it. Whatever moves it comes as a notice of appeal or an order, which the rows' trigger
"an appeal, a stay or a new order in Massachusetts v. Oz" names, or as a motion, which the watch list names.

The rules were read on the Legal Information Institute's pages on 2026-11-04, and none is stored:
[Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frap/rule_4), Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure [6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_6) and [59](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_59),
and [28 U.S.C. 2107](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2107). Massachusetts's legal holidays, which Rule
6(a)(6)(C) brings in, do not include the day after Thanksgiving
([M.G.L. c. 4, § 7, clause Eighteenth](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleI/Chapter4/Section7)).

## Alternatives considered

A dated correction in 0013. 0013 is accepted, and an accepted record is superseded rather than edited.

Leaving 0013 as it stands, since its words are true of the government. The watch list and a row cite 0013 for a time
to appeal that runs for every party, and a reader who follows either citation finds only the government's.

Superseding only the words "The government's", so that the record says every party has until 2026-12-28. A check on
2026-12-29 that found no notice could then call the time over while an extension or a reopening could still come, or
while a notice filed that day had not yet reached RECAP.

A last date after which a check may call the time over, such as 2027-01-28, the day after the last day to move for an
extension. Rule 4(a)(6) can still reopen the time on a motion filed as late as 2027-04-27, and a notice under Rule
4(a)(3) or a motion under Rule 4(a)(4) moves every later date.
