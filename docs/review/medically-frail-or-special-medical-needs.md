## Blind and disabled, as section 1614 of the Social Security Act defines them

### blind-as-defined-in-section-1614

- The rule: Section 1614(a)(2) of the Social Security Act, whose definition 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A) incorporates for an individual who is blind, considers an individual blind if they have central visual acuity of 20/200 or less in the better eye with the use of a correcting lens. An eye with a limitation in the fields of vision such that the widest diameter of the visual field subtends an angle no greater than 20 degrees is considered as having a central visual acuity of 20/200 or less. An individual is also considered blind if they are blind as defined under a State plan approved under title X or XVI of the Act (subchapter X or XVI in the Code) as in effect for October 1972 and received aid under that plan, on the basis of blindness, for December 1973, so long as they are continuously blind as so defined.
- From: [SSA 1614(a)(2)](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/ssact/title16b/1614.htm); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(2)
