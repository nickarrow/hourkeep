# Who the requirement applies to, and who is excluded from it

This file holds the skill's rule items for one topic of the corpus: who the community engagement requirement applies
to, who is excluded from it, and the prohibition of waivers of it in 42 CFR 435.563. Each item restates one row of
`docs/domain/community-engagement-extract.md` in short form, and where an item and its row differ, the row wins.
Paths that begin with docs/ are from the repository root, not from this skill's folder. Each item ends with its
row's identifier in square brackets and the row's citation. In these items, the requirement is the community
engagement requirement, a person it applies to is what the rule calls an applicable individual, and a person the rule
excludes from it is what the rule calls a specified excluded individual. Whether a person is either is the agency's
determination. A person's facts may point to more than one category, such as someone caring for a parent who may be
both a caretaker relative and a family caregiver, and an answer names each.

## Where the requirement applies

- The rules on community engagement, §§ 435.550 to 435.563, apply in the 50 States and the District of Columbia,
  and not in the territories. [requirement-applies-in-the-50-states-and-dc] 42 CFR 435.550; 91 FR 33470
- From the state's implementation date, eligibility under § 435.119, the coverage for people age 19 or older and
  under 65 with income at or below 133 percent FPL, is subject to the requirement.
  [adult-group-eligibility-subject-to-community-engagement] 42 CFR 435.119(d); 91 FR 33469

## Who is an applicable individual

- An applicable individual is someone who is not a specified excluded individual, as § 435.554 defines one, and who
  is described in one of the next two items.
  [applicable-individual-defined] 42 CFR 435.551 introductory text; 91 FR 33470
- Someone eligible to enroll, or enrolled, under the State plan under § 435.119 is an applicable individual unless
  they are a specified excluded individual.
  [applicable-individual-under-the-state-plan] 42 CFR 435.551(a); 91 FR 33470
- Someone otherwise eligible to enroll, or enrolled, in a section 1115 demonstration whose coverage meets minimum
  essential coverage requirements is an applicable individual, unless they are a specified excluded individual, if
  they are at least 19 and under 65, not pregnant, not entitled to or enrolled for Medicare Part A and not enrolled
  for Part B, and not otherwise eligible to enroll under the State plan.
  [applicable-individual-under-a-demonstration] 42 CFR 435.551(b); 91 FR 33470
- The preamble says many section 1115(a)(2) demonstration populations, such as family planning only groups, do not
  meet the definition, some are always excluded, and several could be subject to the requirement; CMS's review
  continues. [demonstration-populations-reviewed-for-community-engagement] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.551(b); 91 FR 33354
- CMS's overview deck of 2026-07-27 says that, to date, 13 populations covered only under section 1115(a)(2)
  demonstrations, in 8 States, are subject to the requirement. Not verified: the deck is not stored.
  [cms-identified-13-demonstration-populations-in-8-states] CMS IFC overview slide deck of 2026-07-27, slide 9

## Who is a specified excluded individual

- Someone who meets one or more of the categories in § 435.554(c) is not an applicable individual, and the
  requirement is not a condition of their eligibility. Whether they meet a category is the agency's determination.
  [specified-excluded-individual-not-subject-to-community-engagement] 42 CFR 435.554(b); 91 FR 33471
- Meeting one of the categories in paragraphs (c)(1) to (c)(10) is enough. The categories are in (c), not (a),
  which holds the definitions. [specified-excluded-individual-meets-one-category] 42 CFR 435.554(c); 91 FR 33471

## Parents, guardians, caretaker relatives and family caregivers

- A parent, guardian, caretaker relative or family caregiver, as § 435.554(a) defines each, is a specified excluded
  individual, and a family caregiver must also meet one of the criteria the paragraph cites as (c)(1)(i)(A) through
  (C), which do not exist; the preamble says the criteria are at (c)(3)(i). Which governs is an open question.
  [parent-guardian-caretaker-relative-or-family-caregiver-excluded]
  42 CFR 435.554(c)(3); 91 FR 33472
- A family caregiver is a specified excluded individual if they meet one of the three criteria that follow.
  [family-caregiver-excluded-on-one-criterion] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(i); 91 FR 33472
- A family caregiver who primarily lives with the dependent child or disabled individual qualifies if the assistance
  they give comes on a regular basis and is not solely incidental in nature.
  [family-caregiver-living-with-care-recipient] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(i)(A); 91 FR 33472
- A family caregiver who is a relative from the caretaker relative list, without its live-with and primary
  responsibility requirements, and does not live with the person qualifies if the assistance comes on a regular basis
  and is not solely incidental in nature. The relatives are those of caretaker-relative-defined; whether the
  relationships a state adds count is an open question. [family-caregiver-relative-living-apart]
  42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(i)(B); 91 FR 33472
- A family caregiver who neither lives with nor is such a relative of the person qualifies only with not less than
  80 hours a month of assistance that is not solely incidental in nature. The relatives are those of
  caretaker-relative-defined; whether the relationships a state adds count is an open question.
  [family-caregiver-unrelated-living-apart-80-hours] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(i)(C); 91 FR 33472
- The preamble says such a caregiver with fewer than 80 hours is an applicable individual unless another exclusion
  applies, and the hours count as unpaid work: with 55 hours, they need 25 more of the listed activities.
  [caregiving-hours-below-80-count-as-unpaid-work] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(i)(C); 91 FR 33371
- More than one parent, guardian, caretaker relative or family caregiver in one residence may each be a specified
  excluded individual. [more-than-one-excluded-in-a-residence] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(ii); 91 FR 33472

## The definitions behind the exclusion for parents, guardians, caretaker relatives and family caregivers

- In § 435.554, a caretaker relative lives with a dependent child or disabled individual, assumes primary
  responsibility for their care, and is their father, mother, grandfather, grandmother, brother, sister, stepfather,
  stepmother, stepbrother, stepsister, uncle, aunt, first cousin, nephew or niece; the disabled individual's husband,
  wife, son, daughter, stepson, stepdaughter, grandson or granddaughter; or the spouse of such a parent or relative,
  even after death or divorce ends the marriage.
  [caretaker-relative-defined] 42 CFR 435.554(a), definition of caretaker relative; 91 FR 33471
- A state may also count as a caretaker relative another relative by blood, half-blood, adoption or marriage, the
  domestic partner of the parent or other caretaker relative, or an adult the person lives with who assumes primary
  responsibility for their care; the elections it made for the group at § 435.110 apply here too. Each, like the
  listed relatives, must live with the dependent child or disabled individual and assume primary responsibility for
  their care, as the definition's opening requires. Name which relationships the state counts.
  [caretaker-relative-other-relationships-at-state-option] 42 CFR 435.554(a),
  definition of caretaker relative, paragraph (iv); 91 FR 33471
- A dependent child is a child 13 years of age or under who relies on another individual for care.
  [dependent-child-defined] 42 CFR 435.554(a), definition of dependent child; 91 FR 33471
- A disabled individual is someone who meets the ADA definition of disability at 28 CFR 35.108, whether or not they
  are eligible for Medicaid or another federal program on the basis of a disability.
  [disabled-individual-defined] 42 CFR 435.554(a), definition of disabled individual; 91 FR 33471
- The preamble says a disabled individual can be of any age, an older adult who needs help with activities of daily
  living included, and from birth, overlapping through age 13 with a dependent child.
  [disabled-individual-has-no-age-limit] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(a); 91 FR 33368; 91 FR 33369
- Under 28 CFR 35.108, disability is a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major
  life activities, a record of such an impairment, or being regarded as having one. How the regarded as prong
  applies to the caregiver exclusion is an open question.
  [ada-disability-definition] 28 CFR 35.108(a)(1); eCFR 2026-10-21
- The ADA definition of disability in 28 CFR 35.108 is construed broadly in favor of expansive coverage.
  [ada-disability-construed-broadly] 28 CFR 35.108(a)(2)(i); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Any one of the three prongs of 28 CFR 35.108 is enough to establish disability, each as the section's later
  paragraphs, in the next group, define and limit it. How the regarded as prong applies to the caregiver exclusion
  is an open question. [ada-disability-any-one-or-more-prongs]
  28 CFR 35.108(a)(2)(ii); eCFR 2026-10-21
- A family caregiver is an adult family member or other individual with a significant relationship with a dependent
  child or disabled individual who provides care within a broad range of assistance to them.
  [family-caregiver-defined] 42 CFR 435.554(a), definition of family caregiver; 91 FR 33471
- A guardian is an adult appointed by a court, under State law, to care for and make personal decisions for a
  dependent child or disabled individual who cannot care for themselves.
  [guardian-defined] 42 CFR 435.554(a), definition of guardian; 91 FR 33471
- A parent is someone with the legal status of mother or father under State law, adoption included, who provides
  some level of care to a dependent child or disabled individual.
  [parent-defined] 42 CFR 435.554(a), definition of parent; 91 FR 33471

## The terms of the ADA definition of disability

- Under 28 CFR 35.108, a physical or mental impairment is any physiological disorder or condition, cosmetic
  disfigurement or anatomical loss affecting one or more body systems, or any mental or psychological disorder; it
  includes, among others, cancer, diabetes, epilepsy, HIV infection, emotional illness, specific learning
  disabilities, drug addiction and alcoholism. [ada-physical-or-mental-impairment-defined]
  28 CFR 35.108(b)(1) and (b)(2); eCFR 2026-10-21
- A physical or mental impairment does not include homosexuality or bisexuality.
  [ada-impairment-excludes-homosexuality-and-bisexuality] 28 CFR 35.108(b)(3); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Major life activities include, among others, caring for oneself, seeing, hearing, walking, learning, thinking,
  communicating and working, and the operation of a major bodily function, such as the immune system or normal cell
  growth. Major is not read strictly, and an activity need not be of central importance to daily life.
  [ada-major-life-activities-defined] 28 CFR 35.108(c)(1) and (c)(2); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Substantially limits is construed broadly in favor of expansive coverage and is not a demanding standard.
  [ada-substantially-limits-construed-broadly] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1) introductory text and (d)(1)(i); eCFR 2026-10-21
- An impairment that substantially limits one major life activity need not limit others.
  [ada-one-major-life-activity-is-enough] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(iii); eCFR 2026-10-21
- An impairment that is episodic or in remission is a disability if it would substantially limit a major life
  activity when active. [ada-episodic-or-in-remission-impairment] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(iv); eCFR 2026-10-21
- An impairment substantially limits when it does so compared with most people in the general population; it need
  not prevent or severely restrict the activity, and not every impairment is a disability.
  [ada-substantially-limits-compared-to-most-people] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(v); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Whether an impairment substantially limits a major life activity takes an individualized assessment, against a
  standard lower than the one applied before the ADA Amendments Act. The assessment is the agency's.
  [ada-substantially-limits-individualized-assessment] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(vi); eCFR 2026-10-21
- The comparison with most people usually will not require scientific, medical or statistical evidence, though such
  evidence may be presented where appropriate.
  [ada-comparison-usually-needs-no-scientific-evidence] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(vii); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Whether an impairment substantially limits is judged without regard to mitigating measures such as medication,
  hearing aids, mobility devices, assistive technology or therapy, except that ordinary eyeglasses or contact lenses
  are considered. [ada-mitigating-measures-not-considered] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(viii) and (d)(4); eCFR 2026-10-21
- The effects of an impairment lasting or expected to last less than six months can be substantially limiting under
  the actual disability and record of prongs, since the six-month transitory part of the transitory and minor
  exception does not apply to them.
  [ada-impairment-under-six-months-can-substantially-limit] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(1)(ix); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Some impairments will in virtually all cases be found to substantially limit a major life activity, such as
  deafness, blindness, intellectual disability, autism, cancer, cerebral palsy, diabetes, epilepsy, HIV infection,
  major depressive disorder and schizophrenia, and for them the individualized assessment should be particularly
  simple and straightforward. The list is examples. The assessment is the agency's.
  [ada-some-impairments-virtually-always-substantially-limit] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(2)(ii) and (d)(2)(iii);
  eCFR 2026-10-21
- In appropriate cases the condition, manner or duration of performing an activity, compared with most people, may
  be considered, side effects of medication and treatment burdens included; the focus is on how an activity is
  limited, not on what outcomes the person achieves. [ada-condition-manner-or-duration] 28 CFR 35.108(d)(3);
  eCFR 2026-10-21
- A record of an impairment is a history of, or a misclassification as having, an impairment that substantially
  limits one or more major life activities, construed broadly. Whether a person has one is the agency's
  determination. [ada-record-of-impairment-defined] 28 CFR 35.108(e)(1) and (e)(2); eCFR 2026-10-21
- A person is regarded as having an impairment if subjected to a prohibited action because of an actual or perceived
  impairment, whether or not it limits a major life activity. How this prong applies to the caregiver exclusion,
  where no prohibited action may be in question, is an open question. [ada-regarded-as-having-impairment]
  28 CFR 35.108(f) introductory text and (f)(1); eCFR 2026-10-21
- A person is not regarded as having an impairment if the public entity demonstrates that it is, objectively, both
  transitory, meaning lasting or expected to last six months or less, and minor.
  [ada-regarded-as-excludes-transitory-and-minor] 28 CFR 35.108(f)(2); eCFR 2026-10-21
- Disability does not include transvestism, transsexualism, pedophilia, exhibitionism, voyeurism, gender identity
  disorders not resulting from physical impairments, or other sexual behavior disorders; compulsive gambling,
  kleptomania or pyromania; or psychoactive substance use disorders resulting from current illegal use of drugs.
  [ada-disability-exclusions] 28 CFR 35.108(g); eCFR 2026-10-21

## Former foster care children and American Indians

- Someone who meets the definition of the group for former foster care children, as the SUPPORT Act amended it, is
  a specified excluded individual, whether or not they turned 18 on or after January 1, 2023.
  [former-foster-care-child-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(1); 91 FR 33472
- The preamble's criteria: under 26; not enrolled in a mandatory group of section 1902(a)(10)(A)(i)(I) to (VII),
  even if eligible for one; in foster care under any State's responsibility at 18, or the higher age the State
  elected in its title IV-E plan; and enrolled in Medicaid in any State while in that care.
  [former-foster-care-criteria-apply-whatever-the-age-18-date] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(1); 91 FR 33365-33366
- Someone who meets the definition of Indian at 42 CFR 447.51 is a specified excluded individual.
  [american-indian-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(2); 91 FR 33472
- Under 42 CFR 447.51, an Indian is a member of a Federally-recognized Indian tribe; someone in an urban center who
  meets one of the next item's criteria; someone the Secretary of the Interior considers an Indian for any purpose;
  or someone the Secretary of Health and Human Services considers an Indian for Indian health care services,
  California Indians, Eskimos, Aleuts and other Alaska Natives included.
  [indian-defined-at-447-51] 42 CFR 447.51, definition of Indian; eCFR 2026-10-21
- Someone in an urban center is an Indian if they are a member of a tribe, band or other organized group of Indians,
  including those terminated since 1940 and those the State recognizes, or a first or second degree descendant of a
  member; an Eskimo, Aleut or other Alaska Native; considered an Indian by the Secretary of the Interior; or
  determined to be one under regulations of the Secretary, meaning the Secretary of Health and Human Services.
  [urban-indian-criteria-at-447-51] 42 CFR 447.51, definition of Indian, paragraph (2); eCFR 2026-10-21;
  42 CFR 400.200, definition of Secretary; eCFR 2026-10-21

## Veterans

- A veteran with a temporary or permanent disability from the Department of Veterans Affairs rated as 100 percent
  (total) under 38 U.S.C. 1155 is a specified excluded individual.
  [veteran-with-total-disability-rating-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(4); 91 FR 33472
- The preamble says a veteran paid 100 percent compensation for individual unemployability, even with a combined
  rating below 100 percent, is treated as one with a combined rating of 100 percent and meets the exclusion.
  [veteran-paid-at-total-rate-for-unemployability-excluded] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(4); 91 FR 33372

## TANF, SNAP and treatment programs

- Someone compliant with the State's requirements under section 407 of the Act, TANF's work requirements, is a
  specified excluded individual. Here the test is compliance.
  [tanf-work-requirement-compliant-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(6); 91 FR 33472
- A member of a household that receives SNAP benefits who is not exempt from a SNAP work requirement is a specified
  excluded individual. The test is being subject to the work rules, not complying with them.
  [snap-household-member-not-exempt-from-work-requirement-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(7); 91 FR 33472
- The preamble says the State need not confirm compliance with SNAP's work rules, and being not exempt from the
  general work requirement, the time limit work requirement, or both is enough.
  [snap-exclusion-covers-general-and-time-limit-work-requirements] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(7); 91 FR 33377
- Someone participating in a drug addiction or alcoholic treatment and rehabilitation program, as the Food and
  Nutrition Act of 2008 defines one, is a specified excluded individual. A state may set a minimum time commitment
  for participation; name whether it has. [treatment-program-participant-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(8);
  91 FR 33472
- The preamble says such a program is one conducted by a private non-profit organization or institution, or a
  publicly operated community mental health center, to provide treatment that can lead to rehabilitation, and States
  decide which programs meet the definition.
  [treatment-program-defined-by-food-and-nutrition-act] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(8); 91 FR 33378

## Inmates, and pregnancy and postpartum

- An inmate of a public institution, as § 435.1010 defines one, is a specified excluded individual.
  [inmate-of-public-institution-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(9); 91 FR 33472
- The preamble says that means someone in custody and held involuntarily through operation of law enforcement, such
  as in a prison, jail or detention facility, and not someone in a medical institution, an institution for mental
  diseases included. [inmate-means-held-involuntarily-in-custody] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(9); 91 FR 33378
- Someone pregnant, or entitled to postpartum medical assistance under section 1902(e)(5) or (16) of the Act, is a
  specified excluded individual. [pregnant-or-postpartum-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(10); 91 FR 33472
- The preamble says postpartum coverage runs to the end of the month in which the 60-day period after the end of
  pregnancy ends, or, where the State elected it, 12 months after the end of pregnancy. Name which the state gives.
  [postpartum-period-runs-from-the-end-of-pregnancy] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(10); 91 FR 33378-33379

## No waivers of the requirement

- CMS will not approve a section 1115 demonstration that waives the community engagement provisions of section
  1902(xx) of the Act, in whole or in part. [no-waiver-of-community-engagement] 42 CFR 435.563(a); 91 FR 33479
- A state that runs the requirement through section 1115 demonstration authority must still ensure compliance with
  each requirement of section 1902(xx) of the Act.
  [demonstration-must-meet-every-community-engagement-requirement] 42 CFR 435.563(b); 91 FR 33479
