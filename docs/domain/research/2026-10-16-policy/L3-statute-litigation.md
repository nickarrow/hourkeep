## B. Six-month renewals, SSA § 1902(e)(14)(L)

- § 71107(a) adds subparagraph (L): redeterminations scheduled on or after the first day of the first quarter after
  December 31, 2026 happen every 6 months for adult-group enrollees and for people described in that group but covered
  by a waiver that gives MEC-equivalent coverage to everyone in it [S·V title7-part3.md:233-242]. The only exemption is
  for individuals described in § 1902(xx)(9)(A)(ii)(II): Indians, Urban Indians, California Indians and the
  IHS-eligible [S·V :244, :704]. "State" means the 50 States and DC [S·V :246]. Guidance was due within 180 days
  [S·V :248]; computed due date December 31, 2025.
- The IFC does not implement it. Revised § 435.916(a)(1) says MAGI renewals happen once every 12 months and no more
  often [R·V 2026-11094.txt:4706-4714, 91 FR 33480]. The preamble explains that the IFC restored the pre-2024 CFR text
  because § 71102 blocks the 2024 eligibility rule [P·V :352-358, 91 FR 33352], and it separately reminds States that
  the adult group moves to 6-month renewals from January 1, 2027, except Indians and § 1115 enrollees outside
  full-expansion waivers [P·V :1835, 91 FR 33398]. The corpus's account of the conflict is accurate.
- CMS sent State Medicaid Directors SMD #26-001 on March 6, 2026, covering the § 71107 change, renewal rules that still
  apply, and operational transition [G·V CMCS GovDelivery bulletin 40cffd3]. Computed: 65 days after the statutory
  deadline. medicaid.gov returned HTTP 403 for the PDF, so the option detail comes from CCF (March 6, 2026) and SHVS
  [2·V]: a State may apply 6 months at each person's next scheduled renewal, or initiate some 2027 renewals early to
  shorten current 12-month periods. Under the second option no renewal starts before January 1, 2027, no period is
  shorter than 6 months, and affected people get advance notice with hearing rights.
- SHVS cites the provision as § 71101. The enrolled text puts it at § 71107; do not copy SHVS's section number.
- Federal Register API: since July 4, 2025 the only CMS documents matching "435.916" or "1902(e)(14)(L)" are the IFC and
  its correction [G·V federalregister.gov/api/v1, queried 2026-10-16].

## D. Massachusetts v. Oz, No. 1:26-cv-12962-RGS (D. Mass.)

- Filed June 29, 2026; Judge Richard G. Stearns [C·V Dkt 1 stamp; Dkt 94]. 26 plaintiffs, counted from the caption in
  Dkt 94: 23 States, DC, and the Governors of Kentucky and Pennsylvania, Colorado among them.
- Complaint. Count I, contrary to law: the frailty gate, the NEA emergency test, and the 2028 attestation limit.
  Count II, arbitrary and capricious, 7 provisions: the gate; the 91 FR 33373 statement that States may not
  automatically classify someone as frail on diagnosis alone; the attestation limit; the 12-month claims window; applicant-month hardship timing
  (§ 435.556(a)(1)-(2)); the NEA test and Stafford duration (§ 435.555(d)(2)(i), (iv)); renewal time limits
  (§ 435.556(a)(2)(i)). Count III, Spending Clause clear notice [F·V Dkt 1 ¶¶ 209-228].
- Preliminary injunction. Hearing July 28; denied without prejudice July 29, on irreparable harm only. The court said
  the denial does not signal its merits view, promised merits briefing before January 1, 2027, and left the States free
  to renew if briefing slips past December 31, 2026 without a federal deferral [C·V Dkt 94 at 2-6].
- Narrowing. The partial summary-judgment motion moves on Counts I and II only and drops the hardship claims; the
  memorandum presses 3 provisions [F·V Dkt 103 at 1-2 and n.1]. The government reads Count I as reduced to the gate,
  the contrary-to-law attestation theory as abandoned, and Count III as reserved, and cross-moved on all three counts
  [F·V Dkt 138-1, ECF pp. 14, 34-35].
- Proposed order [F·V Dkt 103-1]: declare unlawful and vacate (i) the gate phrase in § 435.554(c)(5)(i) [R·V
  2026-11094.txt:3987, 91 FR 33472]; (ii) the 12-month limits in § 435.557(a)(vii), (a)(viii) and (f); (iii) "Before
  January 1, 2028" in § 435.557(f)(1)(i) and all of (f)(1)(ii); (iv) the 91 FR 33373 statement. Enjoin applying them to
  the Plaintiff States, and bar penalizing those States for implementation errors or delays for 6 months. The States
  seek vacatur as to everyone subject to the rule, citing a First Circuit decision of August 7, 2026 [F·V Dkt 104,
  ECF p. 36].
- Government [F·V Dkt 138-1, ECF pp. 38-41; Dkt 142]: "(as defined by the Secretary)" is an express delegation under
  Loper Bright; any explanatory defect is curable on remand; asks for remand without vacatur and no injunction; notes
  that universal vacatur would reach non-plaintiff States.
- Schedule. Briefing closed with the government's reply on October 7 (Dkt 142). The hearing first set for October 20 at
  2:00 PM (notice entered August 6) was reset to October 22 at 10:00 AM, 40 minutes a side (Dkt 143, entered October 8)
  [C·V]. RECAP shows no later entry and no termination [C·V CourtListener docket 73546826, queried 2026-10-16].
- § 435.557(b)(2)(ii), the general documentation rule from January 1, 2028, is not challenged in either suit; both target
  only the frailty-specific limit in § 435.557(f) [F·V Dkt 1 ¶ 214, ¶ 220(c); Taylor Dkt 1 ¶¶ 101-102, ¶ 195(d)].

  statute id 57021]. Applicants show the month before applying; enrollees show 3 months since their last
  determination; the cabinet may not seek a good-faith exemption without the General Assembly; notice by September 1,

  Governor is a Massachusetts v. Oz plaintiff.

  13, 2026; bill text not read]. The State Board of Medical Services adopts rules by January 1, 2027, written for the
  least administrative burden; HCPF publishes monthly eligibility and enrollment data on the requirement's effects from
  March 1, 2027; the duties lapse if the federal requirement is amended, relocated or repealed.

  calendar month before applying; renewing members show 1 calendar month since the last renewal deadline; 2027
