# Notices of noncompliance and fair hearings

This file holds the skill's rule items for one topic of the corpus: notices of noncompliance, fair hearings, keeping
coverage during an appeal, and renewals already scheduled in 2027. Each item restates one row of
`docs/domain/community-engagement-extract.md` in short form, and where an item and its row differ, the row wins.
Paths that begin with docs/ are from the repository root, not from this skill's folder. Each item ends with its
row's identifier in square brackets and the row's citation. In these items, the requirement is the community
engagement requirement, and a person it applies to is what the rule calls an applicable individual.

A state makes separate choices about when the notice of noncompliance comes: one at renewal, and one for checks
between renewals if it makes those checks. CMS's explanation of the rule, the preamble, calls the two renewal choices
Option 1 and Option 2, and calls its two choices for checks between renewals Option 1 and Option 2 as well. CMS's
letter SMD 26-001 calls its two transition paths for renewals already scheduled in 2027 Option 1 and Option 2 too.
They are different choices, so this file names each by what it does.

## When the state sends a notice of noncompliance

- When the state is unable to verify, in the sense the rule defines, that a person the requirement applies to has met
  it or is deemed to have met it, the state must send them a notice of noncompliance.
  [send-notice-of-noncompliance] 42 CFR 435.558(a)(1); 91 FR 39030
- At application, the state is unable to verify when the information the person gave and the reliable information it
  has are not enough to show they met, or are deemed to have met, the requirement for the number of months its State
  plan requires. [unable-to-verify-at-application] 42 CFR 435.558(b)(1); 91 FR 39030-39031
- The preamble says a state may accept an applicant's declaration under penalty of perjury on the application that
  they do not meet the requirement during the review period under § 435.556(a)(1), without further verification,
  and treat their noncompliance as verified. It is a state option, at application only, and the preamble does not
  say whether a notice of noncompliance still follows.
  [accept-applicant-declaration-of-noncompliance] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(1); 91 FR 33411

## At renewal, in a state that sends the notice with the renewal form

These items apply only in a state that chose to send the notice of noncompliance with the renewal form, which it
records in its State plan; where the plan records no choice, no stored source says which renewal choice applies, which
is an open question in `docs/domain/open-questions.md`.

- At renewal, in a state that sends the notice of noncompliance with the renewal form, the state is unable to verify,
  for the number of months its State plan requires, once the reliable information it accesses at renewal is not
  enough. [renewal-notice-with-renewal-form] 42 CFR 435.558(b)(2)(i); 91 FR 39031
- The preamble says that in a state that sends the notice of noncompliance with the renewal form, once the reliable
  information the state accesses at renewal is not enough, the state must send the notice at the same time as the
  pre-populated renewal form. [notice-sent-with-renewal-form] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(2)(i); 91 FR 33411

## At renewal, in a state that sends the notice after the renewal form

These items apply only in a state that chose to send the notice of noncompliance after the renewal form: once the
form comes back without enough information, or once the time allowed for returning it has passed, whichever comes
first. The state records its choice in its State plan; where the plan records no choice, no stored source says which
renewal choice applies, which is an open question in `docs/domain/open-questions.md`.

- At renewal, in a state that sends the notice of noncompliance after the renewal form, the state is unable to verify,
  for the number of months its State plan requires, after the renewal form, sent to people whose eligibility cannot be
  renewed from reliable information under § 435.916(a)(2), is not returned or comes back without enough information.
  [renewal-notice-after-renewal-form-period] 42 CFR 435.558(b)(2)(ii); 91 FR 39031
- The preamble says that in a state that sends the notice of noncompliance after the renewal form, the
  state would send the notice after the time it allowed for returning the renewal form or, if earlier, after the
  person returned the form without enough information.
  [notice-sent-after-renewal-form-period] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(2)(ii); 91 FR 33412
- The preamble says a state that sends the notice of noncompliance after the renewal form would need to
  allow for the extra time its renewals take, because it gives the person two separate periods of at least 30
  calendar days to provide information, one with the pre-populated renewal form and one with the notice.
  [renewal-form-and-notice-give-two-periods] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(2)(ii); 91 FR 33412
- The preamble says that in a state that sends the notice of noncompliance after the renewal form, the
  notice is required only when community engagement is the only factor of eligibility left to verify after the time
  allowed for returning the renewal form has passed. Where the
  form also asked about other factors and the person did not return it, or returned it without that other
  information, the state must give notice and fair hearing rights before disenrolling them for procedural reasons,
  and should tell them that returning the form during the reconsideration period may get their eligibility
  reconsidered, and how they may show community engagement during that period.
  [no-noncompliance-notice-when-other-factors-unanswered] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(2)(ii); 91 FR 33412

## Between renewals, in a state that sends the notice straight after its data check

These items apply only in a state that checks compliance more often than at renewal and chose to send the notice of
noncompliance straight after its data check; where a state that makes those checks has made no choice, no stored
source says which choice for checks between renewals applies, which is an open question in
`docs/domain/open-questions.md`.

- In a state that checks compliance more often than at renewal and sends the notice of noncompliance straight after
  its data check, the state is unable to verify, for the number of months its State plan requires, once the reliable
  information it accesses is not enough.
  [more-frequent-check-notice-after-data-check] 42 CFR 435.558(b)(3)(i); 91 FR 39031
- The preamble says that in a state that checks compliance more often than at renewal and sends the notice of
  noncompliance straight after its data check, the state must send the notice once it is unable to verify, does not
  need to send a separate request for information first, and the notice's 30 calendar days meet the duty to seek more
  information before ending eligibility on information the state received.
  [no-separate-request-before-notice-after-data-check] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(3)(i); 91 FR 33412

## Between renewals, in a state that asks for information first

These items apply only in a state that checks compliance more often than at renewal and chose to ask the person for
information before sending the notice of noncompliance; where a state that makes those checks has made no choice, no
stored source says which choice for checks between renewals applies, which is an open question in
`docs/domain/open-questions.md`.

- In a state that checks compliance more often than at renewal and asks the person for information before sending
  the notice of noncompliance, the state is unable to verify, for the number of months its State plan requires, after
  the reliable information it accesses is not enough, it asks the person for information under its procedures, and
  what it asked for is not returned or is not enough.
  [more-frequent-check-notice-after-information-request] 42 CFR 435.558(b)(3)(ii); 91 FR 39031
- The preamble says that in a state that checks compliance more often than at renewal and asks the person for
  information before sending the notice of noncompliance, if the person does not answer that request or does not
  send enough, the state must then send the notice and give the 30 calendar days to make a satisfactory showing.
  [notice-after-unanswered-information-request] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.558(b)(3)(ii); 91 FR 33412-33413

## What the notice must say

- The notice of noncompliance must clearly say how to show the person met the requirement or should be deemed to have
  met it, including which month or months the state will assess.
  [notice-says-how-to-show-compliance] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(i)(A)-(C); 91 FR 39031
- The notice of noncompliance must clearly say how to show the requirement does not apply to the person, because they
  are not an applicable individual, including because they fall in a specified excluded group.
  [notice-says-how-to-show-not-applicable] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(ii); 91 FR 39031
- The notice of noncompliance must clearly state the deadline for giving the state the information that shows the
  person met the requirement, should be deemed to have met it, or is someone the requirement does not apply to.
  [notice-states-the-deadline] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(iii); 91 FR 39031
- The notice of noncompliance must describe how the person may send the state the information that shows they met
  the requirement, should be deemed to have met it, or are someone the requirement does not apply to, through any of
  the ways the regulation it cross-refers to lists. [notice-says-how-to-submit] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(iv); 91 FR 39031
- The notice of noncompliance must describe what not meeting the requirement, and not answering the notice, mean for
  Medicaid and for the premium tax credit and its advance payments used to pay for Marketplace coverage.
  [notice-says-consequences-including-premium-tax-credit] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(v); 91 FR 39031
- The notice of noncompliance must clearly say how the person may reapply for Medicaid if their application is denied
  or they are disenrolled. [notice-says-how-to-reapply] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(vi); 91 FR 39031
- In a state that has elected the short-term hardship exception, the notice of noncompliance must include the
  information about short-term hardship events the regulation describes.
  [notice-gives-hardship-information-where-elected] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(1)(vii); 91 FR 39031
- The notice of noncompliance must be provided in a manner consistent with the general notice rule the regulation
  cross-refers to, which the extract does not yet cover.
  [notice-follows-section-435-905b] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(2); 91 FR 39031
- A notice of noncompliance sent electronically must comply with the electronic notice rule the regulation
  cross-refers to, which the extract does not yet cover.
  [electronic-notice-follows-section-435-918b] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(3); 91 FR 39031

## The 30 days to answer a notice of noncompliance

- The state must give the person 30 calendar days to make a satisfactory showing, beginning on the date they receive
  the notice of noncompliance. The notice counts as received 5 days after the date on it, unless the person shows
  they did not receive it within those 5 days. [notice-response-period] 42 CFR 435.558(a)(2); 91 FR 39030
- The notice of noncompliance counts as received 5 days after the date on it, unless the person shows they did not
  receive it within the 5-day period. [notice-presumed-received-after-5-days] 42 CFR 435.558(c)(4); 91 FR 39031
- Within the 30 calendar days to answer a notice of noncompliance, the person may show they met the requirement, or
  should be deemed to have met it, including through the short-term hardship exception where the state offers it, for
  each month the State plan requires. [showing-of-compliance-or-exception] 42 CFR 435.558(a)(2)(i); 91 FR 39030
- Within the 30 calendar days to answer a notice of noncompliance, the person may, instead of showing they met the
  requirement, show that it does not apply to them, because they are not an applicable individual, including because
  they fall in a specified excluded group.
  [showing-requirement-does-not-apply] 42 CFR 435.558(a)(2)(ii); 91 FR 39030
- A person already enrolled keeps getting Medicaid until they are determined ineligible in the way the regulation
  requires. [coverage-continues-during-noncompliance] 42 CFR 435.558(a)(3); 91 FR 39030

## When the 30 days end without a satisfactory showing

- If the person makes no satisfactory showing within the 30 calendar days a notice of noncompliance gives them, the
  state must consider every other basis of eligibility before denying coverage at application or finding the person
  ineligible. [consider-all-other-bases-before-denial] 42 CFR 435.558(d)(1); 91 FR 39031
- If an applicant makes no satisfactory showing within the 30 calendar days a notice of noncompliance gives them, and
  is found ineligible after all bases are considered, the application is denied, with written notice and fair
  hearing rights. [deny-application-with-notice-and-hearing-rights] 42 CFR 435.558(d)(2)(i); 91 FR 39031
- If an enrolled person makes no satisfactory showing within the 30 calendar days a notice of noncompliance gives
  them, and is found ineligible after all bases are considered, they are disenrolled no later than the end of the
  month after the month the 30 calendar days end, and only after advance written notice and fair hearing rights.
  [disenroll-with-advance-notice-and-hearing-rights] 42 CFR 435.558(d)(2)(ii); 91 FR 39031
- If the person makes no satisfactory showing within the 30 calendar days a notice of noncompliance gives them, the
  specific reasons in the notice of the denial or disenrollment must say the person failed both to show they met the
  requirement, or should be deemed to have met it, for the months assessed, and to show the requirement does not
  apply to them. [adverse-notice-names-both-failures] 42 CFR 435.558(d)(2)(iii)(A)-(B); 91 FR 39031
- If the person makes no satisfactory showing within the 30 calendar days a notice of noncompliance gives them, and
  is found ineligible after all bases are considered, the state must determine whether they may be eligible for
  other insurance affordability programs.
  [check-other-insurance-affordability-programs] 42 CFR 435.558(d)(2)(iv); 91 FR 39031
- The state may not restrict a person's ability to reapply, or to be covered if found eligible on reapplication,
  because of an earlier denial or disenrollment for noncompliance.
  [no-restriction-on-reapplying] 42 CFR 435.558(e); 91 FR 39031
- A person enrolled on MAGI-based eligibility who was disenrolled for not sending information a notice of
  noncompliance asked for, and who sends it during the reconsideration period, must have their eligibility
  reconsidered. The period is 90 days after the date of termination, or longer if the state elects.
  [reconsideration-after-disenrollment] 42 CFR 435.558(f); 91 FR 39031; 42 CFR 435.916(a)(3)(iii); 91 FR 33481

## Hearing rights and the notice of action

Some items below and in Keeping coverage during an appeal, from 42 CFR 431.211, 431.230 and 431.231, depend on
sections the corpus has not read. The rules in these items are stored and were read. What is missing is the
definition of the date of action, in 42 CFR 431.201, and the exceptions to the 10 days of advance notice
[advance-notice-at-least-10-days], in 42 CFR 431.213 and 431.214; the 5-day notice is the one required under 42 CFR
431.214. A notice of action has to state the date the action takes effect
[notice-of-action-states-action-and-effective-date], but the corpus has not confirmed that this is the date of action
42 CFR 431.201 defines, and it cannot say when either exception applies. When an answer turns on the date of action
or on an exception, say so, point the person to the dates on their own notice, and name the agency as the one to ask.

- The agency must issue and publicize its hearing procedures.
  [publicize-hearing-procedures] 42 CFR 431.206(a); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency must tell every applicant and beneficiary in writing of their right to a fair hearing and to ask for an
  expedited one, how to get a hearing, that they may represent themselves or use legal counsel, a relative, a friend,
  or other spokesman, and the time frames for the agency's final action.
  [hearing-rights-information] 42 CFR 431.206(b); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency must give the written information about fair hearing rights when a person applies for Medicaid.
  [hearing-rights-at-application] 42 CFR 431.206(c)(1); eCFR 2026-10-15
- Besides giving it at application, the agency must give the written information about fair hearing rights when it
  denies a claim for eligibility, benefits or services, denies a request for exemption from mandatory enrollment in
  an Alternative Benefit Plan, takes other action as the regulations define it, or whenever a hearing is otherwise
  required. [hearing-rights-at-denial-or-action] 42 CFR 431.206(c)(2); eCFR 2026-10-15
- Where the agency has delegated its fair hearings to the Exchange or an Exchange appeals entity, it must tell the
  person in writing that they may have their hearing before the agency instead, and how to choose that.
  [hearing-before-agency-instead-of-exchange] 42 CFR 431.206(d); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The information about fair hearing rights must be accessible to people with limited English proficiency and to
  people with disabilities, and may be given electronically.
  [hearing-information-accessible] 42 CFR 431.206(e); eCFR 2026-10-15
- A notice of action required under § 431.206(c)(2), (c)(3) or (c)(4) must say what action the agency, skilled
  nursing facility or nursing facility intends to take and when it takes effect.
  [notice-of-action-states-action-and-effective-date] 42 CFR 431.210(a); eCFR 2026-10-15
- A notice of action required under § 431.206(c)(2), (c)(3) or (c)(4) must clearly state the specific reasons for
  the intended action. [notice-of-action-states-specific-reasons] 42 CFR 431.210(b); eCFR 2026-10-15
- A notice of action required under § 431.206(c)(2), (c)(3) or (c)(4) must give the specific regulations that
  support the action, or the change in federal or state law that requires it.
  [notice-of-action-cites-regulations] 42 CFR 431.210(c); eCFR 2026-10-15
- A notice of action required under § 431.206(c)(2), (c)(3) or (c)(4) must explain the person's right to a local
  evidentiary hearing if there is one, or a State agency hearing, or, where the action is based on a change in law, when
  a hearing will be granted. [notice-of-action-explains-hearing-right] 42 CFR 431.210(d); eCFR 2026-10-15
- A notice of action required under § 431.206(c)(2), (c)(3) or (c)(4) must explain when Medicaid continues if a
  hearing is requested. [notice-of-action-explains-continued-coverage] 42 CFR 431.210(e); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency must send a notice of action at least 10 days before the date of action, except where the regulations
  allow otherwise. [advance-notice-at-least-10-days] 42 CFR 431.211; eCFR 2026-10-15

## Asking for a hearing

- The State agency must grant an opportunity for a hearing to anyone who asks because they believe it acted wrongly,
  denied their claim for eligibility or for covered benefits or services, issued a determination of their liability,
  or did not act on the claim with reasonable promptness.
  [hearing-required-on-request] 42 CFR 431.220(a)(1); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency need not grant a hearing if the only issue is a federal or state law that requires an automatic change
  adversely affecting some or all beneficiaries.
  [hearing-not-required-when-only-issue-is-automatic-change-in-law] 42 CFR 431.220(b); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency must let a person, or their authorized representative, ask for a hearing by any of the ways the
  regulations list; the online, telephone and other electronic ways have their own effective date.
  [hearing-request-by-any-modality] 42 CFR 431.221(a)(1)(i); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency must let a person, or their authorized representative, include a request for an expedited hearing in
  their hearing request. [expedited-hearing-request] 42 CFR 431.221(a)(1)(ii); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency may not limit or interfere with a person's freedom to ask for a hearing.
  [no-interference-with-hearing-request] 42 CFR 431.221(b); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency may help a person submit and process their hearing request.
  [state-may-help-with-hearing-request] 42 CFR 431.221(c); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency must allow a reasonable time to ask for a hearing, not to exceed 90 days from the date the notice of
  action is mailed. The state sets the time within that limit.
  [hearing-request-window-at-most-90-days] 42 CFR 431.221(d); eCFR 2026-10-15

## Keeping coverage during an appeal

The items here that use the date of action or the 5-day notice depend on sections the corpus has not read, which
the paragraph at the start of Hearing rights and the notice of action explains.

- If the agency sent the notice of action 10 days in advance, or the 5-day notice, and the person asks for a hearing
  before the date of action, the agency may not end or reduce services until the hearing decision, unless the
  hearing finds the only issue is one of federal or state law or policy and the agency promptly tells the person in
  writing. [services-maintained-when-hearing-requested-before-action] 42 CFR 431.230(a); eCFR 2026-10-15
- If the hearing decision upholds the agency's action, the agency may seek to recover the cost of services the person
  received only because services continued while they waited for the hearing decision.
  [recovery-if-action-upheld] 42 CFR 431.230(b); eCFR 2026-10-15
- The agency may reinstate services if a person asks for a hearing not more than 10 days after the date of action.
  [agency-may-reinstate-within-10-days-after-action] 42 CFR 431.231(a); eCFR 2026-10-15
- Services the agency reinstates after a hearing request must continue until the hearing decision, unless the
  hearing finds the only issue is one of federal or state law or policy.
  [reinstated-services-continue-until-decision] 42 CFR 431.231(b); eCFR 2026-10-15
- If the agency acted without the required advance notice, the person asks for a hearing within 10 days of receiving
  the notice of action, and the agency determines the action came from something other than applying federal or
  state law or policy, it must reinstate and continue services until the hearing decision. The notice counts as
  received 5 days after its date unless the person shows that they did not receive it within those 5 days.
  [must-reinstate-after-action-without-advance-notice] 42 CFR 431.231(c); eCFR 2026-10-15
- If a person's whereabouts were unknown because agency mail came back unforwardable, discontinued services must be
  reinstated if their whereabouts become known while they are eligible. The paragraph of the regulation requiring this
  sunsets on October 1, 2034.
  [reinstate-when-whereabouts-become-known] 42 CFR 431.231(d); 91 FR 33469

## Renewals every 6 months, and renewals already scheduled in 2027 (CMS letter SMD 26-001)

These items cover who is outside the 6-month renewals that begin in 2027 and the people already enrolled in a 12-month
eligibility period in the adult expansion group on January 1, 2027, whose annual renewals were already scheduled for
2027; for those people a state takes one of two paths, which SMD 26-001 calls "Reschedule Renewal Initiation Dates for
Beneficiaries Enrolled as of January 1, 2027" and "Transition Beneficiaries Enrolled on January 1, 2027, at Their Next
Scheduled Renewal".

- The statute's requirement that a state redetermine eligibility once every 6 months does not apply to an individual
  who is an Indian or an Urban Indian as the Indian Health Care Improvement Act defines those terms, a California
  Indian described in section 809(a) of that Act, or someone otherwise determined eligible as an Indian for the Indian
  Health Service. [six-month-renewals-do-not-apply-to-exempt-american-indians-and-alaska-natives]
  SSA 1902(e)(14)(L)(ii); Pub. L. 119-21, sec. 71107
- For people already enrolled in a 12-month eligibility period in the adult expansion group on January 1, 2027, whose
  renewals are already scheduled in 2027, CMS says a state may move the renewal earlier to give as close to a 6-month
  eligibility period as possible from the effective date of their last eligibility determination, but no earlier
  than January 1, 2027, which CMS says is practically January 4, 2027, and give a 6-month period at that renewal if
  the person is still eligible in the adult expansion group. By the
  adult expansion group CMS means the people subject to 6-month renewals under the state plan or a demonstration,
  which leaves out certain American Indians and Alaska Natives.
  [reschedule-2027-renewals-to-six-months] CMS SMD 26-001, pp. 4, 7-8
- For people already enrolled in a 12-month eligibility period in the adult expansion group on January 1, 2027, whose
  renewals are already scheduled in 2027, CMS says a state may keep the 12-month period and the renewal already
  scheduled in 2027, rather than move the renewal earlier, giving a 6-month period at that renewal to those who stay
  eligible in the adult expansion group. By the adult expansion group CMS means the people subject to 6-month
  renewals under the state plan or a demonstration, which leaves out certain American Indians and Alaska Natives.
  [keep-scheduled-2027-renewals] CMS SMD 26-001, pp. 4, 7, 8, 10
- A state that moves renewals already scheduled in 2027 earlier should not shorten the previously approved
  eligibility period of a person in the adult expansion group as of January 1, 2027 to less than 6 months, because
  that would renew eligibility more often than once every 6 months. By the adult expansion group CMS means the people
  subject to 6-month renewals under the state plan or a demonstration, which leaves out certain American Indians and
  Alaska Natives. [first-path-period-not-shorter-than-six-months] CMS SMD 26-001, pp. 4, 8
- CMS considers shortening an eligibility period an action, so a state that moves renewals already scheduled in 2027
  earlier must tell the people previously granted a 12-month eligibility period of the change and give them at least
  10 days' advance notice with fair hearing rights.
  [shortened-renewal-period-needs-advance-notice] CMS SMD 26-001, p. 9
