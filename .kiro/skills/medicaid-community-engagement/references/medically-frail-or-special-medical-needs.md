# People who are medically frail or have special medical needs

This file holds the skill's rule items for one topic of the corpus: the exclusion in 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5) for a
person who is medically frail or otherwise has special medical needs, what a federal court ordered about it on
2026-10-29, the definitions of blind and disabled in section 1614 of the Social Security Act that it incorporates, the
State's list of conditions, and how the State verifies the status under 42 CFR 435.557(f). Each item restates one row
of `docs/domain/community-engagement-extract.md` in short form, and where an item and its row differ, the row wins.
Paths that begin with docs/ are from the repository root, not from this skill's folder. Each item ends with its row's
identifier in square brackets and the row's citation. In these items, the requirement is the community engagement
requirement, and a person the rule excludes from it is what the rule calls a specified excluded individual.

On 2026-10-29, in Massachusetts v. Oz, a federal court vacated and set aside the words of the definition that require
a person's condition to significantly impair their ability to comply with the requirement, and enjoined the
defendants, CMS and HHS and the officials heading them, and anyone acting for them or with them from applying those
words to the states that sued, Colorado among them. Since then the definition is in force without those words
in those states, so a person in one of its five groups is medically frail there. Whether the same holds in the states
that did not sue is an open question. The rows on the definition quote its text as printed, and the items say what is
in force. Whether a person is medically frail, and whether what they send is enough to show it, is the agency's
determination. A person's facts may point to more than one group, or to another exclusion as well, and an answer
names each.

## The exclusion and its definition

- A person who is medically frail or otherwise has special medical needs, as § 435.554(c)(5) sets out, is a
  specified excluded individual. Whether they are is the agency's determination.
  [medically-frail-or-special-medical-needs-excluded] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5); 91 FR 33472
- As printed, the definition requires a physical, mental or other behavioral health condition that significantly
  impairs the person's ability to comply with the requirement, and that the person be in one of the five groups. On
  2026-10-29 a federal court vacated and set aside those impairment words and enjoined the defendants, CMS and HHS and
  the officials heading them in their official capacities, their agents, employees, appointees, successors and anyone
  acting in concert or participation with them from applying the words to the states that sued, Colorado among them, so
  in those states a person in one of the five groups is medically frail. Whether the vacatur, which names no party, has
  the same effect in the states that did not sue, in CMS's practice, is an open question. [medically-frail-defined]
  42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i); 91 FR 33472; Massachusetts v. Oz, D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2

## What the court ordered on 2026-10-29

Two sources conflict here, and the conflict is an open question: six-months-without-penalty-for-plaintiff-states cites
entry 148 and entry 147 on what the six months reach.

- In Massachusetts v. Oz, the federal court in Massachusetts declared unlawful, and vacated and set aside under
  5 U.S.C. 706(2), the words that require a condition significantly impairing a person's ability to comply, and
  enjoined the defendants, CMS and HHS and the officials heading them in their official capacities, their agents,
  employees, appointees, successors and anyone acting in concert or participation with them from applying those
  words to the plaintiff states, Colorado among them. The vacatur names no party, the injunction names the plaintiff
  states, and the order states no end date for either. The court held that the five groups the statute names set a floor
  CMS lacked authority to narrow by excluding any subset of the people in them. Whether the vacatur reaches the states
  that did not sue, in CMS's practice, is an open question. [medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated] Massachusetts v.
  Oz, D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2; docket entry 147, pp. 1-2, 9, 11
- The order bars the defendants, CMS and HHS and the officials heading them in their official capacities, their
  agents, employees, appointees, successors and anyone acting in concert or participation with them from penalizing
  the plaintiff states, Colorado among them, for any errors or delays in implementing the rule for six months from
  the order. The memorandum says those states should receive a 6-month stay of enforcement for any determination of
  whether a person is medically frail. The order reaches errors or delays in
  implementing the whole rule, and the memorandum reaches those determinations; which of the two describes the six
  months is an open question.
  Neither gives an end date, and the memorandum says the stay has no impact on the January 1, 2027 deadline for
  implementation. [six-months-without-penalty-for-plaintiff-states] Massachusetts v. Oz, D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962,
  docket entry 148, p. 2; docket entry 147, pp. 1-2, 15-16

## The five groups, and how CMS and the preamble read two of them

- A person who is blind or disabled, as section 1614 of the Social Security Act defines each, is in one of the five
  groups. The definition's opening binds this group. As printed, it also requires the impairment words, which a
  federal court vacated and set aside on 2026-10-29, as medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated records.
  [medically-frail-blind-or-disabled] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A); 91 FR 33472; Massachusetts v. Oz, D. Mass. No.
  1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2
- A person with a substance use disorder is in one of the five groups, except a person in stable recovery, meaning in
  recovery for 5 or more years. The definition's opening binds this group. As printed, it also requires the impairment
  words, which a federal court vacated and set aside on 2026-10-29, as medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated
  records. [medically-frail-substance-use-disorder] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(B); 91 FR 33472; Massachusetts v. Oz,
  D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2
- CMS's frailty deck reads stable recovery in § 435.554(c)(5)(i)(B) as recovery from a substance use disorder for 5
  or more consecutive years, so such a person would not be medically frail under that group. The regulation says in
  recovery for 5 or more years. [stable-recovery-read-as-five-consecutive-years] CMS, Implementing Medical Frailty
  Under Community Engagement, slide 31
- A person with a disabling mental disorder is in one of the five groups. The definition's opening binds this group.
  As printed, it also requires the impairment words, which a federal court vacated and set aside on 2026-10-29, as
  medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated records. [medically-frail-disabling-mental-disorder]
  42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(C); 91 FR 33472; Massachusetts v. Oz, D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2
- The preamble says it would be reasonable for States to consider these conditions, when they are disabling and
  significantly impair a person's ability to comply with the requirement, as disabling mental disorders:
  schizophrenia, schizotypal disorder, delusional disorder, other non-mood psychotic disorders, moderate or severe
  bipolar disorder, major depressive disorder and panic disorder. The list is examples, not an exhaustive list. On
  2026-10-29 a federal court vacated and set aside the definition's words on which the second of those conditions rests,
  as medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated records. The preamble says the ISMICC, DSM-5 and ICD-10 criteria for
  serious mental illness are commonly used to define and classify disabling mental disorders and may help States set
  criteria. A state chooses its criteria for identifying a disabling mental disorder; name the criteria it uses.
  [disabling-mental-disorder-examples] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(C); 91 FR 33375; Massachusetts v. Oz,
  D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2
- A person with a physical, intellectual or developmental disability that significantly impairs their ability to
  perform one or more activities of daily living is in one of the five groups. The definition's opening binds this
  group. As printed, it also requires the impairment words, which a federal court vacated and set aside on
  2026-10-29, as medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated records.
  [medically-frail-disability-impairing-daily-living] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(D); 91 FR 33472; Massachusetts v. Oz,
  D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2
- A person with a serious or complex medical condition is in one of the five groups. Such a condition is life
  threatening; seriously disabling without necessarily being life threatening; causes significant pain or discomfort
  that can cause serious interruptions to life activities; requires a major time or effort commitment from caregivers
  for a substantial period of time; requires frequent monitoring; is associated with severe consequences or negative
  consequences for someone else; affects multiple organ systems; requires management to tight physiological
  parameters; requires coordination of multiple specialties; requires treatment that carries a risk of serious
  complications; or requires adjustment in non-medical environments. The definition's opening binds this group. As
  printed, it also requires the impairment words, which a federal court vacated and set aside on 2026-10-29, as
  medically-frail-impairment-phrase-vacated records. [medically-frail-serious-or-complex-condition]
  42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)(E); 91 FR 33472; Massachusetts v. Oz, D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-12962, docket entry 148, pp. 1-2

## Blind and disabled, as section 1614 of the Social Security Act defines them

- Under section 1614(a)(2), whose definition § 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A) incorporates, a person is blind with central
  visual acuity of 20/200 or less in the better eye with a correcting lens. An eye whose visual field is limited so
  that its widest diameter subtends an angle of no more than 20 degrees counts as having central visual acuity of
  20/200 or less. A person is also blind if they are blind as defined under a State plan approved under title X or
  XVI as in effect for October 1972 and received aid under that plan, on the basis of blindness, for December 1973,
  so long as they stay continuously blind as so defined.
  [blind-as-defined-in-section-1614] SSA 1614(a)(2); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(2)
- Under section 1614(a)(3)(A) and (B), whose definition § 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A) incorporates, a person is disabled if
  they are unable to engage in any substantial gainful activity because of a medically determinable physical or
  mental impairment that can be expected to result in death or has lasted or can be expected to last for a
  continuous period of not less than twelve months. They are disabled only if the impairment or impairments are so
  severe that, considering their age, education and work experience, they can do neither their previous work nor any
  other kind of substantial gainful work which exists in the national economy, whether or not such work exists in
  the area where they live, a specific job vacancy exists for them, or they would be hired if they applied. Work
  which exists in the national economy means work which exists in significant numbers in the region where the person
  lives or in several regions of the country.
  [disabled-as-defined-in-section-1614] SSA 1614(a)(3)(A); SSA 1614(a)(3)(B); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(3)(A)-(B)
- For section 1614(a)(3), a physical or mental impairment is one that results from anatomical, physiological or
  psychological abnormalities which are demonstrable by medically acceptable clinical and laboratory diagnostic
  techniques. [disabling-impairment-medically-demonstrable] SSA 1614(a)(3)(D); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(3)(D)
- The Commissioner of Social Security prescribes by regulation when services performed or earnings derived from them
  show an ability to engage in substantial gainful activity, and a person whose services or earnings meet those
  criteria is found not to be disabled, notwithstanding section 1614(a)(3)(B). Where a disability is severe enough
  that the person needs assistance to work, their earnings exclude the cost to them of attendant care services,
  medical devices, equipment, prostheses and similar items and services needed for that work, as the Commissioner's
  regulations determine, whether or not the assistance is also needed for their normal daily functions, and within
  reasonable limits the Commissioner may set. Routine drugs and routine medical services are not excluded unless
  needed to control the disabling condition. Whether the activity is legal plays no part.
  [substantial-gainful-activity-criteria] SSA 1614(a)(3)(E); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(3)(E)
- Notwithstanding section 1614(a)(3)(A) through (E), a person is also disabled for title XVI if they are
  permanently and totally disabled as defined under a State plan approved under title XIV or XVI as in effect for
  October 1972 and received aid under that plan, on the basis of disability, for December 1973 and for at least one
  month before July 1973, so long as they stay continuously disabled as so defined.
  [disabled-under-former-state-plan] SSA 1614(a)(3)(F); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(3)(F)
- In deciding whether a person's impairments are medically severe enough to be the basis of eligibility under
  section 1614, the Commissioner of Social Security considers the combined effect of all of them, whether or not
  any one alone would be that severe. If the Commissioner finds a medically severe combination, its combined impact
  is considered throughout the disability determination process.
  [combined-effect-of-impairments-considered] SSA 1614(a)(3)(G); 42 U.S.C. 1382c(a)(3)(G)
- In determinations of disability under title XVI, 42 U.S.C. 421(h), 421(k) and 423(d)(5) apply as they do under
  title II. A person eligible for title XVI benefits by reason of disability for the month before the month they turn
  18 has that eligibility redetermined by the criteria for initial eligibility for people age 18 or older, either
  during the 1-year period beginning on their 18th birthday or, in place of a continuing disability review, whenever
  the Commissioner of Social Security decides their case is subject to it; paragraph (4) does not apply to it.
  [title-ii-disability-rules-apply-to-section-1614] SSA 1614(a)(3)(H)(i); SSA 1614(a)(3)(H)(iii); 42 U.S.C.
  1382c(a)(3)(H)
- Notwithstanding section 1614(a)(3)(A), a person is not disabled for title XVI if alcoholism or drug addiction
  would, but for this rule, be a contributing factor material to the Commissioner of Social Security's determination
  that they are disabled. [drug-addiction-or-alcoholism-material-not-disabled] SSA 1614(a)(3)(J); 42 U.S.C.
  1382c(a)(3)(J)

## The State's list of conditions, and asking to be considered

Two sources conflict here, and the conflict is an open question: state-develops-list-of-frailty-conditions and
frailty-condition-list-auditable-and-consistent cite § 435.554(c)(5)(ii)'s paragraphs (a)(5)(i)(A) through (E), which do
not exist, and the preamble's § 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A) through (E).

- The State must develop a list of diseases, diagnoses, disorders or other health conditions to identify people who
  meet the criteria of the five groups. The list identifies people, and where the state has none, the five groups of
  § 435.554(c)(5)(i) still say who is medically frail. The paragraph cites the criteria as paragraphs (a)(5)(i)(A)
  through (E), which do not exist, and the preamble names § 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A) through (E); the two conflict, and
  which governs is an open question. A state chooses which conditions its list holds.
  [state-develops-list-of-frailty-conditions] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(ii); 91 FR 33472
- The State's list must be auditable, justifiable and consistent with the definitions of the five groups. The
  paragraph cites those definitions as paragraphs (a)(5)(i)(A) through (E), which do not exist, and the preamble names
  § 435.554(c)(5)(i)(A) through (E); the two conflict, and which governs is an open question.
  [frailty-condition-list-auditable-and-consistent] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(ii)(A); 91 FR 33472
- The State must revise its list on a regular basis, adding or removing conditions based on its experience applying
  the exclusion. A state chooses how often it revises the list; the rule sets no interval.
  [frailty-condition-list-revised-regularly] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(ii)(B); 91 FR 33472
- A person whose disease, diagnosis, disorder or health condition is not on the State's list can still ask to be
  considered: the State must have reasonable processes and criteria in place for them to request consideration for
  the exclusion. A state chooses its process and criteria; name them where the state has published them.
  [request-consideration-when-condition-not-listed] 42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(ii)(C); 91 FR 33472

## How the State verifies medical frailty

- The agency must first attempt to verify that a person is medically frail from reliable information available to
  the State, including claims relevant to the person adjudicated in the preceding 12 months, whether paid, pended or
  denied, and encounter data, as relevant to them.
  [frailty-verified-first-from-reliable-information] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1); 91 FR 39030
- The preamble says that when a State's initial screening questions identify a person as potentially medically frail,
  the State must attempt to verify it from reliable information available to the State before seeking documentation
  or other information from the person. From January 1, 2028, at the renewal after a status verified by a statement
  under penalty of perjury, and at each verification after it, States must attempt it from adjudicated claims or
  encounter data, as relevant to the person, for the preceding 12 months before requesting documentation from them.
  States should consider plain language
  screening questions at application and renewal; a state chooses whether to use them.
  [frailty-checked-in-state-records-before-person-asked] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1); 91 FR 33405; 91 FR 33407
- CMS's frailty deck says States may wish to look first at exclusions for which reliable information is readily
  available in the State's system, such as being a parent of a dependent child 13 years of age or under, pregnancy or
  American Indian status, before exclusions that need other data sources or are more likely to need documentation,
  which may include medical frailty. It encourages a process that minimizes administrative burden, weighing the
  availability of data and the likelihood that a person has documentation reasonably available. A state chooses the
  order in which it checks the exclusions.
  [state-may-check-exclusions-in-own-records-first] CMS, Implementing Medical Frailty Under Community Engagement,
  slide 10
- The preamble says States may not consider information older than 12 months when verifying medical frailty,
  because older information may not reflect the person's current condition.
  [frailty-verification-uses-no-information-older-than-12-months] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1); 91 FR 33405
- The preamble says the absence of claims or encounter data altogether, or of particular claims or types of claims,
  may not be used to find a person ineligible for the exclusion; a person may not be found not medically frail only
  because their condition or use of care relates to diagnosis codes not on a list; and a State unable to verify the
  status from its own information must give the person the opportunity to provide documentation or other
  information. [missing-claims-cannot-defeat-frailty-exclusion] Preamble to 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1); 91 FR 33406
- Before January 1, 2028, when the State has no reliable information, or it is not reasonably compatible with what
  the person or someone on their behalf provided, the agency may require documentation, or accept a statement or
  other information under penalty of perjury, sufficient as the State determines, each time it verifies the status.
  A state chooses which it requires or accepts, and what is sufficient; the rule names no default.
  [frailty-documentation-or-sworn-statement-before-2028] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1)(i); 91 FR 39030
- CMS's frailty deck says the documentation a State may seek from a person to verify whether they are medically
  frail may include, and is not limited to, health records such as medical history, treatment records, progress
  notes, discharge paperwork, medication lists and lab or other diagnostic test results; provider documentation or
  certification; managed care plan care management information; and level of care or functional assessment
  information not in the State's administrative data. A state chooses which documentation it seeks. When a State
  may require documentation is in the regulation's items on documentation and sworn statements.
  [documents-state-may-seek-for-frailty] CMS, Implementing Medical Frailty Under Community Engagement, slide 20
- The preamble says a person's status may be apparent from other documentation or information they provide, even
  when they do not identify themselves on a screener, and that States may accept provider documentation from many
  types of practitioners qualified under State scope of practice laws, including physicians, nurse practitioners,
  physician assistants, psychologists, counselors and therapists, clinical social workers and other practitioners the
  State credentials. A State's list of qualified practitioners must be shared with CMS on request. A state chooses
  whose documentation it accepts. [frailty-documentation-from-many-kinds-of-practitioner] Preamble to
  42 CFR 435.557(f)(1); 91 FR 33406

## Statements under penalty of perjury from 2028, and verifying again

- Beginning on January 1, 2028, when the State has no reliable information, or it is not reasonably compatible with
  what the person or someone on their behalf provided, the agency may accept a statement or other information under
  penalty of perjury, sufficient as the State determines, to verify the exclusion only once during the person's
  period of enrollment, as § 435.557(a) defines it. A state chooses whether to accept one and what is sufficient,
  and the limit of once per period of enrollment holds whatever it chooses.
  [one-sworn-statement-per-period-of-enrollment-from-2028] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1)(ii); 91 FR 39030
- From January 1, 2028, at the person's first regularly scheduled redetermination after their status was determined
  from their statement under penalty of perjury or other information, the agency must verify the status from
  reliable information available to the State or, if that is not sufficient, from documentation submitted by or on
  behalf of the person. [frailty-verified-at-first-renewal-after-sworn-statement] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1)(ii)(A);
  91 FR 39030
- If a person claims the status again during the same period of enrollment, after having sought it on or after
  January 1, 2028 on the basis of a statement under penalty of perjury or other information, the agency must verify
  it from reliable information available to the State or, if that is not sufficient, from documentation submitted by
  or on behalf of the person. [second-frailty-claim-after-sworn-statement-needs-other-proof]
  42 CFR 435.557(f)(1)(ii)(B); 91 FR 39030
- After verifying the status from reliable information available to the State or from documentation, the agency must
  verify it again at least every 12 months. A state may choose to do it more often, such as at each renewal.
  [frailty-reverified-at-least-every-12-months] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(1)(iii); 91 FR 39030

## Privacy of the data used to verify

- When accessing, storing and handling data obtained to verify that a person is medically frail or otherwise has
  special medical needs, the agency must comply with all applicable Federal privacy requirements, including section
  1902(a)(7) of the Act; 42 CFR part 431, subpart F; the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996,
  42 U.S.C. 1320d et seq.; 42 CFR part 2; and any other applicable Federal privacy laws.
  [frailty-verification-data-privacy] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(2); 91 FR 39030
- The same Federal privacy requirements apply to data obtained to verify that a person is participating in a drug
  addiction or alcoholic treatment and rehabilitation program, the exclusion of § 435.554(c)(8): section 1902(a)(7)
  of the Act; 42 CFR part 431, subpart F; the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, 42 U.S.C.
  1320d et seq.; 42 CFR part 2; and any other applicable Federal privacy laws.
  [treatment-program-verification-data-privacy] 42 CFR 435.557(f)(2); 91 FR 39030
