### In Colorado, do the 10 days for keeping coverage on appeal run to the member's request or to the Office of Administrative Courts' receipt of it?

- Who can answer: Colorado's HCPF, or the Office of Administrative Courts
- Raised: 2026-10-17, by row agency-may-reinstate-within-10-days-after-action and the Colorado fact for it
- Source: docs/domain/sources/colorado/10-ccr-2505-10-8.000-effective-2026-07-10.txt:2924 "Where the member requests a hearing not more than ten (10) days after"
- Source: docs/domain/sources/colorado/hcpf-appeals-2026-10-17.html:1148 "get your request for an appeal no later than <strong>10 days</strong> after the date of action"
- Source: docs/domain/sources/colorado/hcpf-appeals-2026-10-17.html:1150 "Your coverage will continue until a final decision is made on your appeal."
- Source: docs/domain/sources/ecfr/2026-10-15/42-cfr-431.231.xml:4 "if a beneficiary requests a hearing not more than 10 days after the date of action"
- Note: The two Colorado sources conflict. 10 CCR 2505-10 § 8.057.5.D, like 42 CFR 431.231(a), counts to the
  member's request; HCPF's page counts to the Office of Administrative Courts' receipt. It does so under its heading
  "Eligibility appeals" (hcpf-appeals-2026-10-17.html:1140), where a current Health First Colorado member (:1143)
  whose request the Office of Administrative Courts gets no later than 10 days after the date of action (:1148)
  keeps coverage until a final decision (:1150), and a person applying for the first time who does not qualify can
  appeal but gets no continuation of benefits (:1151). It does so again under "Benefits appeals" (:1152), which
  covers a service previously authorized and then stopped or reduced (:1153 and :1154). An appeal of a disenrollment
  for noncompliance is read here as an eligibility appeal; the page does not name community engagement. Getting it
  wrong is dangerous: a person who reads the rule's words as counting to the day they send the request may be late by
  the page's. The Colorado fact uses the rule's words.

### For the adult expansion group from 2027, which governs how often eligibility is renewed: the regulation's 12 months or the statute's 6?

- Who can answer: CMS
- Raised: 2026-10-18, by rows reschedule-2027-renewals-to-six-months, keep-scheduled-2027-renewals and
  first-path-period-not-shorter-than-six-months
- Source: docs/domain/cms-2454-ifc/2026-11094.xml:4237 "must be renewed once every 12 months, and no more frequently than once every 12 months"
- Source: docs/domain/hr1/119hr1enr-title7-part3.md:238 "a State shall make such a redetermination once every 6 months for the following individuals"
- Source: docs/domain/sources/cms/smd-26-001.txt:180 "from once every 12 months to once every 6 months"
- Note: The two conflict. 42 CFR 435.916(a)(1), as the interim final rule reprints it (91 FR 33480), says the
  eligibility of beneficiaries whose financial eligibility is determined using MAGI-based income must be renewed
  once every 12 months, and no more frequently than once every 12 months, except as its paragraph (d) provides. SSA
  1902(e)(14)(L)(i), added by Pub. L. 119-21, sec. 71107, has a State make such a redetermination once every 6
  months, for redeterminations scheduled on or after the first day of the first quarter that begins after December
  31, 2026, for two groups: individuals enrolled under subsection (a)(10)(A)(i)(VIII), and individuals described in
  that subsection who are otherwise enrolled under a waiver that provides coverage equivalent to minimum essential
  coverage to all individuals described in it (hr1/119hr1enr-title7-part3.md:240 and :242). Clause (ii) exempts any
  individual described in subsection (xx)(9)(A)(ii)(II) (:244), whom SMD 26-001 describes as certain American
  Indians and Alaska Natives (smd-26-001.txt:133 to :143, page 4). The heading uses SMD 26-001's term: the letter
  refers to the individuals subject to 6-month renewals under either the state plan or a demonstration collectively
  as the adult expansion group (smd-26-001.txt:156 to :157). SMD 26-001 says the statute changes the frequency
  from once every 12 months to once every 6 months and did not amend the steps states must take to complete
  renewals under CMS regulations, and that states must continue to complete renewals as § 435.916 describes. The
  preamble says most of the population required to demonstrate community engagement is also subject to a new
  6-month renewal requirement (docs/domain/cms-2454-ifc/2026-11094.xml:1806, 91 FR 33398), and in its next sentence
  that the 6-month renewal requirement does not apply to American Indians or to individuals enrolled in section
  1115 demonstrations, except for those that cover certain adults eligible under a section 1115 demonstration that
  provides MEC to all individuals who would be eligible if the State provided coverage to the adult group under the
  State plan, and that for these individuals eligibility must be renewed every 12 months (same line). Which
  demonstrations pass that whole-group test is research open question 10
  (docs/domain/research/2026-10-16-policy/ledger.md:229). No stored source says how the regulation's text is
  reconciled with the statute, and § 435.916(d) was not read. The stored statute is a derived
  copy whose origin was never recorded. The epic design names this as the first conflict for this list.
